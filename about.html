I Was Always Going To End Up Here

I was the kid reading the back of the bottle rather than the front — the front is advertising, the back is where the information lives, when there is any.

I chased down studies the way other kids collected trading cards. I tested things on myself long before anyone had a word for it, with fairly mixed results. But the habit stuck: find the paper, read the paper, then decide.

A lot of it didn't hold up. Claims tended to shrink once you found the research behind them, when there was research behind them at all. That habit turned into a career, and more than a decade of practising pharmacy in Ontario later, I still read the paper before I believe anything.

The Part That Bothered Me

Somewhere along the way I started noticing how wound up everyone had become. Not from any professional vantage point — just from paying attention. Friends, family, people my age and younger.

Permanently half-braced for judgement. Measuring themselves against a feed that only ever shows everyone else's best day. More hours on a screen than in a room with real people — and then the room happens, and it lands hard.

And it shows up right before the things that count. The presentation. The first date. The interview. The room full of strangers you're expected to work.

Nobody Had Built The Middle Option

Look at what's actually available. Alcohol helps briefly, then quietly costs you the sharpness you came for. Energy drinks add stimulation to a system that's already running hot. Herbal calmers work by slowing you down, which isn't much use twenty minutes before you need to be quick.

Every option asked for the same trade: give up sharpness to get calm, or calm to get sharpness. I wanted both, and nobody was making it.

So I built it. Five compounds, each one there for a stated reason. L-Theanine for alpha-wave support, backed by fifty randomised trials. Motherwort, which binds the GABA-A receptor — the brain's primary inhibitory brake. Acetyl L-Tyrosine for catecholamine availability under load. Uridine Monophosphate for synaptic support. And a small, deliberate amount of caffeine, buffered so it never becomes the problem.

No proprietary blend. No twelfth ingredient at a dose too small to matter. If I couldn't explain why something was in the bottle, it didn't go in — and every paper behind it is linked on the science page, so you can read them yourself.
