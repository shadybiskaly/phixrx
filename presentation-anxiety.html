Command The Room.

You've done the work. The deck is tight, the numbers hold up, and you know this material better than anyone in the room. Now you just need your body to cooperate for the next twenty minutes.

Preparation Isn't The Variable

Here's what nobody tells you about presenting: the preparation was never the hard part. You can rehearse an opening forty times and still feel it slip the moment you stand up — because what changes when you stand up isn't your knowledge. It's your physiology.

Your autonomic nervous system runs one program for high-stakes situations, and it was written long before boardrooms. Adrenaline releases. Heart rate climbs. Blood flow shifts toward your extremities on the assumption you're about to move fast — which means slightly less of it reaching the prefrontal cortex holding everything you rehearsed.

That's why breathing exercises only take you so far, and why the large coffee in the lobby was a mistake. This is a physical event, and it responds to physical inputs. Which is good news — it means it's something you can actually prepare for.

What's In the Bottle, and Why It's There

This is the scenario the formula was built around. One 100ml shot, roughly thirty minutes before you're up:

  • Sceletium Extract — 25mg. In a 2020 trial they gave people a single dose and then made them give a speech. Subjective anxiety was lower before the stressor, and heart rate moved too. That is not an analogy for this page. It is this page.
  • L-Theanine — 200mg, the amount used in the stress research, in the same 100ml of water. Studied for supporting alpha-wave activity: relaxed and completely awake, which is exactly the state you want at the front of a room.
  • Saffron Extract + micro-dosed caffeine — cortisol tends to peak somewhere between the introduction and your second sentence. Saffron is in here for that curve; the 50mg of caffeine is there because in liquid it peaks around the thirty-minute mark, which is when you're standing up.
