Deployment Protocol 04

"Just Be Yourself."

Genuinely excellent advice. Completely useless at 120 beats per minute. Because the version of you that shows up when your sympathetic nervous system is running the show is not, in any meaningful sense, yourself.

The Cruel Joke of Caring

Here's the cruel part. The more you want it to go well, the harder your body works against you. Caring registers as stakes, stakes register as threat, and your sympathetic nervous system responds by preparing you for a fight nobody scheduled.

The result reads badly from across a table. Physical tension looks like disinterest. Overthinking looks like distraction. You're gripping a glass slightly too hard and processing your last sentence instead of hearing theirs — and the entire time you're the most invested person at that table.

So they meet a rigid, distracted stranger, and you go home and decide you're bad at this. You're not bad at this. You were just heavily sedated by your own adrenal glands.

What's In the Bottle, and Why It's There

One shot, thirty minutes before you meet:

  • Motherwort — GABA-A receptor binding. Your posture and your face take their cues from your pulse, and both are considerably more legible when it isn't racing.
  • Acetyl L-Tyrosine — precursor to the neurotransmitters that timing and wit run on. The ones stress spends first.
  • None of the fog — the loose, easy version of yourself people chase with a pre-date drink, without spending the sharpness that makes you good company.
