Deployment Protocol 03

Two Hundred Name Tags.

You know exactly three people here and one of them is already deep in conversation. There's a cash bar, because there's always a cash bar, and it is about to become the single worst decision available to you.

Why Two Hours Feels Like Eight

Because you're not just talking. You're running a continuous background process — where do I put my hands, is this going on too long, is he looking for someone better to talk to, what was her name, what was her name — and that process is expensive. Hyper-vigilance burns real cognitive fuel, which is why you can leave a two-hour event feeling like you did a day's work.

And the cash bar? The first drink genuinely helps. So does the second, a bit. Somewhere in the third, the thing that was loosening you up starts eroding the exact faculties you came here to use — the name recall, the judgement about what's appropriate, the ability to stop talking. You've traded sharpness for ease, and at a professional event that's a bad trade at any price.

What's In the Bottle, and Why It's There

One shot before you go and write your own name on a sticker:

  • Uridine Monophosphate — feeds the CDP-choline pathway your neurons build membranes from. In here for verbal recall, which is the first thing to go in a crowded room.
  • L-Theanine + micro-dosed caffeine — the pairing with fifty randomised trials behind it. Enough lift to stay engaged for the third hour, without the edge caffeine brings on its own.
  • Nothing to regret at 7am — you keep your judgement, your name recall, and every conversation you actually had.
